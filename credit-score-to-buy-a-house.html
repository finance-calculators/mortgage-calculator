What Credit Score Do You Need to Buy a House in 2025?

7 min read · Updated June 2025
The minimum credit score to buy a house depends on your loan type — as low as 500 for FHA loans. But the score you need to get a good rate is much higher. Here's exactly what lenders look for in 2025.

What Makes Up Your Credit Score

  • Payment history (35%) — Most important. Never miss a payment.
  • Credit utilization (30%) — Keep balances below 10% of credit limits
  • Length of credit history (15%) — Older accounts help
  • Credit mix (10%) — Having different types of credit
  • New credit inquiries (10%) — Don't apply for new credit before buying

How to Improve Your Credit Score Before Buying

Quick Wins (1-3 months)

  • Pay down credit card balances — Getting utilization below 10% can add 20-50 points quickly
  • Dispute errors on your credit report — 1 in 5 reports has errors. Check at annualcreditreport.com
  • Become an authorized user — Get added to a family member's old account with good history
  • Don't close old accounts — Keeping them open maintains your credit history length

Medium-Term Improvements (3-6 months)

  • Pay all bills on time — Set up autopay for minimums
  • Ask for credit limit increases — Lowers your utilization ratio
  • Pay off collections — Negotiate to have them removed from your report

Should You Wait to Improve Your Score?

⚠️ The Math of Waiting

If your score is 660 and you could improve to 740 in 6 months, here's the calculation:

  • Buy now at 660 score: $2,154/month on $300k loan
  • Wait 6 months, buy at 740 score: $1,896/month
  • Monthly savings: $258/month
  • Over 30 years: $92,880 saved

6 months of waiting could save you nearly $93,000. Unless home prices rise significantly during that time, improving your score first is almost always worth it.

What Else Lenders Look At

Credit score is important, but lenders also evaluate:

  • Debt-to-income ratio (DTI) — Total monthly debt payments ÷ gross monthly income. Should be below 43%
  • Employment history — 2+ years at same employer preferred
  • Down payment — Larger down payment can offset a lower credit score
  • Cash reserves — Having 2-3 months of mortgage payments in savings shows stability
  • Type of credit — Mortgage lenders look at all three credit bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion)

✅ Pro Tip: Get Pre-Approved First

Before shopping for homes, get pre-approved by 2-3 lenders. This tells you exactly what rate you'll get and how much you can borrow. Multiple mortgage inquiries within 14-45 days count as one hard inquiry — so shopping around doesn't hurt your score.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the minimum credit score to buy a house?

The minimum credit score to buy a house is 500 for FHA loans (with 10% down payment) or 580 for FHA loans with 3.5% down. For conventional loans, the minimum is typically 620. However, getting a good interest rate requires a score of 700-740 or higher.

Can I buy a house with a 600 credit score?

Yes, you can buy a house with a 600 credit score using an FHA loan. However, your interest rate will be significantly higher than someone with a 740+ score. On a $300,000 mortgage, a 600 score could cost you $300-400 more per month and over $100,000 more in total interest compared to a 740 score.

How long does it take to improve your credit score enough to buy a house?

If your score is in the 600-650 range, you can typically improve it to 680-700 in 3-6 months by paying down credit card balances and fixing any errors. Getting from 650 to 740+ usually takes 12-24 months of consistent on-time payments and low utilization.

Does applying for a mortgage hurt your credit score?

A mortgage application results in a hard inquiry that may lower your score by 5-10 points temporarily. However, if you shop with multiple lenders within a 14-45 day window, all those inquiries count as just one. Your score typically recovers within a few months.

What credit score do I need for a conventional loan?

The minimum credit score for a conventional loan is 620. However, to avoid paying Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) and to get the best rates, you typically need a score of 740 or higher. With 20% down and a 740+ score, you'll qualify for the most competitive mortgage rates available.
